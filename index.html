How I Think

The way I see products

Not just what I have built — but the principles behind every decision I make.

Anticipate Before You Build
I design products by mapping what could go wrong before writing a single requirement. If I cannot articulate the edge cases upfront, the feature is not ready to build yet.
The 5-Minute Claim
Every insurance product I build is tested against one question: can a stressed, non-tech-savvy worker complete this in under 5 minutes? If not, it is not done — regardless of how elegant the backend is.
Invisible Complexity
The best insurance product feels simple. The hardest PM work is hiding the compliance, the APIs, the edge cases — so the user only ever sees clarity on the other side.
ProActive, Not Reactive
Insurance has trained users to chase. I build products that chase users instead — proactive status updates, zero-chase experiences. If a user has to call to find out what is happening, the product has not done its job.
Automation as Dignity
When a gig worker cannot afford to wait 3 weeks for a claim, automation is not just efficiency — it is dignity. That is the product bar I hold. Speed of resolution matters to real people in real situations.
Ground Truth Over Assumptions
Every PRD I write is informed by having been in the room when requirements broke down and edge cases were not handled. I write for the entire system — not just the happy path.

The Human
"13 years of knowing how products get delivered. Now using that to decide what gets built — and why."

For 13 years, I had one of the rarest seats in any product organisation — full visibility into what gets built, how it gets built, and whether it actually works for real users.

I sat at the intersection of business intent and technical reality, and watched — sprint after sprint — the same patterns repeat. Workers filing compensation claims and getting silence for weeks. Gig workers told their annual plan did not cover "that kind of work." Claims teams doing their best inside systems that were never designed around the person on the other end.

I kept asking a question that nobody seemed to want to answer: why are these gaps being discovered after delivery, and not designed away at the start?

That question became DaySure. That question became SafeFirst. I am not a PM who picked insurance because it is a growing sector. I am a PM who spent 13 years learning exactly where insurance lets real people down — and decided to build something better.
